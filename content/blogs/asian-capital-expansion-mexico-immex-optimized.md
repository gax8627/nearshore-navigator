## 1. Why Asian Capital is Flooding Into Mexican Manufacturing

The landscape of global manufacturing underwent a structural realignment in 2026. For two decades, multinational OEMs relied on Asian labor arbitrage. However, rising coastal wages in Tier-1 Asian cities, compounding tariffs, and geopolitical instability have reshaped executive decision-making.

### A. Tariff Mitigation and Trade Policy Shields
The main trigger for Asian FDI in Mexico is duty mitigation. US Section 301 tariffs on Chinese-origin goods levy penalties ranging from 7.5% up to 100% on strategic industrial sectors (electronics, semiconductors, EV batteries, and metal mechanics). 

By setting up manufacturing operations in Mexico and satisfying USMCA Regional Value Content (RVC) rules, Asian manufacturers convert finished goods into USMCA-originating products, securing **0% duty access** into the United States market.

### B. Transit Time and Freight Parity
Ocean transit from Shenzhen or Ningbo to the Port of Los Angeles averages 18 to 35 days, exposing shipments to port delays, vessel blank sailings, and freight rate spikes. Conversely, full truckload (FTL) transit from industrial centers like [Tijuana](/en/locations/tijuana) across the Otay Mesa Port of Entry directly into US logistics hubs takes **between 12 and 24 hours**.

### C. Labor Cost Parity and Technical Specialization
With fully burdened assembly wages in Eastern China reaching $7.00–$8.50/hour, the cost delta between Asian production and Mexican assembly has reached parity. Fully burdened operator rates in Northern Mexico average ~$7.84/hour, while providing decades of specialized industrial expertise in medical devices, electronics, and automotive components.

## 2. Unpacking the IMMEX Program for Foreign Investors

Mexico's **IMMEX Program** (*Industria Manufacturera, Maquiladora y de Servicios de Exportación*) represents the centerpiece of Mexico's foreign investment incentives.

### Primary Financial Incentives of IMMEX
1. **Duty-Free Temporary Importation:** Raw materials, components, assembly kits, and packaging materials enter Mexico without triggering General Import Duty (IGI), provided finished goods are re-exported within statutory limits (typically 18 months).
2. **0% IVA (VAT) Exemption:** Through **VAT/IVA Certification** (Annex 28/30), certified IMMEX entities obtain a 100% tax credit against Mexico's standard 16% Value-Added Tax assessed at customs import.
3. **Capital Machinery Exemption:** Tooling, dies, molds, robotics, and testing units can remain in Mexico duty-free throughout the active lifecycle of the IMMEX program.

### Essential Compliance Mechanisms
To retain IMMEX privileges, companies must operate under strict regulatory supervision overseen by Mexico's Tax Administration Service (SAT) and Economy Secretariat (SE):

- **Annex 24 Automated Inventory Control:** Real-time software tracking raw material import manifests against finished export declarations (*pedimentos*).
- **Annex 30 E-Manifest Reporting:** Monthly electronic submissions to SAT proving raw material balances and export reconciliation.
- **Minimum Export Requirements:** Maintenance of at least $500,000 USD in annual export volume or 10% total sales revenue.

## 5. Trade Compliance, USMCA Rules of Origin, and Customs Management

Capitalizing on IMMEX privileges requires rigorous alignment between cross-border trade legalities and operational execution:

1. **Substantial Transformation & Tariff Shift:** Non-USMCA raw materials imported from Asia must undergo tariff classification shifts or meet prescribed Regional Value Content (RVC) percentages to qualify for 0% USMCA preference.
2. **Sub-Maquila Operations:** Licensed IMMEX enterprises can subcontract specialized industrial processes (heat treating, plating, surface finishing) to local Mexican vendors without triggering VAT or import duty penalties.
3. **Cross-Border Customs Clearance:** Managing two distinct legal jurisdictions requires synchronized Mexican *pedimento* filings and US Customs & Border Protection (CBP Form 7501) entries. ## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the IMMEX program in Mexico?
The IMMEX program (formerly Maquiladora) allows foreign-owned manufacturers to temporarily import raw materials, components, and machinery into Mexico duty-free and VAT-free (0% IVA), provided the final products are exported within required statutory timeframes.

### Can Asian companies hold 100% foreign ownership of an IMMEX facility in Mexico?
Yes, Mexican foreign investment law permits 100% foreign ownership of Mexican corporate entities (S. de R.L. or S.A. de C.V.) operating under the IMMEX framework.

### How does the IMMEX program interact with USMCA tariffs?
IMMEX waives Mexican import tariffs on temporary imports. To enter the US duty-free under USMCA preferences, the final product must fulfill USMCA Rules of Origin (such as Tariff Shift or Regional Value Content requirements).

### What is the fastest way for an Asian manufacturer to launch operations in Mexico?
Partnering with a licensed Mexican [Shelter Service](/en/services/shelter-services) provider allows Asian enterprises to launch manufacturing in 60 to 90 days by leveraging the shelter provider's existing IMMEX license, 0% IVA certification, and legal/labor infrastructure.

### Why is Tijuana the preferred location for Asian capital expansion in Mexico?
[Tijuana](/en/locations/tijuana) hosts over 600 export assembly facilities and offers immediate truck drayage (12-24 hours) to Southern California ports, robust Class A industrial supply, and a mature electronics and medical device talent pool.

### What cross-border customs services are mandatory for IMMEX operations?
IMMEX operations require certified [customs brokerage](/en/services/customs-brokerage) to manage Annex 24 inventory control, Annex 30 monthly SAT reporting, pedimento filings, US Customs entry clearance (CBP Form 7501), and cross-border drayage.
