# Tijuana Industrial Park Vacancies & Power Drops 2026: Real Estate & CFE Infrastructure Guide

## Executive Summary: The Dual Challenge of Space & Power in 2026

As North American nearshoring accelerates into 2026, Baja California remains the premier destination for foreign direct investment (FDI) in Mexico. Driven by immediate border access to California, real-time Pacific Standard Time (PST) co-location, and USMCA duty exemptions, major OEMs in medical device manufacturing, aerospace, electronics, and automotive parts continue expanding operations across [Tijuana](/en/locations/tijuana) and surrounding Baja industrial hubs.

However, site selection committees face two interconnected operational variables: **Class A real estate vacancy** and **CFE electrical power drop availability**. 

```

  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  | Real Estate & Infrastructure KPI  | Market Benchmark / Status (2026)                  |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  | Class A Vacancy Rate (Tijuana)   | 2.0% – 3.5% (Normalized from 0.2% in 2023)        |
  | Class A Lease Rates (Tijuana)     | $0.78 – $0.88 NNN / sq ft / month                 |

  | Standard Spec Building Power Drop | 100 KVA – 250 KVA per 50,000 sq ft shell           |

  | CFE Substation Lead Time          | 12 – 18 Months for new high-voltage connections   |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
```

While Class A industrial vacancy in Tijuana has stabilized to **2.0% – 3.5%** in 2026 (offering a healthy relief valve compared to historical 0.2% lows in 2022–2024), **electrical power availability is now the primary gating constraint for plant commissioning**. A 100,000 sq ft speculative building may be physically ready for occupancy within 30 days, but securing a 2 MVA to 5 MVA electrical drop from the Comisión Federal de Electricidad (CFE) can delay production by 12 to 18 months if the park lacks pre-allocated high-voltage infrastructure.

This guide provides executive decision-makers with a comprehensive analysis of industrial park vacancies, lease costs, CFE grid tariffs, and power drop strategies across Tijuana and Baja California.

## 1. Tijuana Industrial Real Estate Overview (2026)

Tijuana's industrial real estate footprint spans over **85 million square feet** of inventory distributed across six strategic submarkets: Otay Mesa, El Florido, Pacifico, Nordika, Playas de Rosarito corridor, and Valle Bonito.

### Industrial Vacancy & Delivery Trends

In 2026, Tijuana's industrial market is experiencing a healthier equilibrium between supply and demand:

- **Class A Vacancy Rate:** Current market vacancy stands between **2.0% and 3.5%**. This represents significant relief compared to the historic inventory drought of 2022-2024 when vacancy hovered below 0.5%.
- **Speculative Construction Pipeline:** Regional developers delivered over 4.2 million sq ft of new Class A inventory in late 2025 and early 2026, primarily within the El Florido, Valle Bonito, and Pacifico expansion corridors.
- **Absorption Velocity:** Premium facilities above 100,000 sq ft equipped with verified >1.5 MVA power capacity are absorbed within 60 to 90 days of breaking ground.

### Class A Lease Rates & NNN Terms

Industrial lease rates in Tijuana reflect sustained premium demand due to direct proximity to the Otay Mesa and San Ysidro ports of entry:

- **Base Rent:** $0.78 to $0.88 NNN per sq ft per month ($9.36 to $10.56 per sq ft annually).
- **Operating Expenses (CAM):** $0.08 to $0.14 per sq ft per month, covering 24/7 park security, common area maintenance, fire suppression infrastructure, property taxes, and insurance.
- **Lease Commitments:** Standard tenant agreements for Class A space require 5 to 10-year lease terms, backed by corporate parent guarantees.

## 3. CFE Electrical Power Drops: The Critical Gating Factor

While real estate availability has improved, electrical power capacity is the #1 cause of unexpected facility commissioning delays for manufacturing operations expanding into Mexico.

### Understanding CFE Power Drop Classifications

The Federal Electricity Commission (CFE - *Comisión Federal de Electricidad*) categorizes industrial electrical service into three main voltage tiers:

1. **Low Voltage (BT - Baja Tensión):** <1 kV. Intended for administrative offices, light retail, and small commercial suites.
2. **Medium Voltage (MT / GDMTH - Gran Demanda en Media Tensión Horaria):** 13.8 kV to 34.5 kV. Standard supply for light assembly, warehouse packaging, and small-scale operations (100 KVA to 1,000 KVA / 1 MVA).
3. **High Voltage (AT - Alta Tensión):** 69 kV to 115 kV+. Essential for heavy manufacturing, plastic injection molding, metal stamping, cleanroom HVAC environments, and automated assembly plants requiring >2 MVA to 50 MVA+.

```
                    POWER ALLOCATION SPECTRUM FOR BAJA INDUSTRIAL FACILITIES

  Light Assembly / Warehouse:  [ 100 - 250 KVA ] --------> Standard Spec Building Base Capacity
  Electronics Assembly:         [ 500 - 1,000 KVA ] ------> Medium Voltage Drop (GDMTH Tariff)
  Medical Device Cleanrooms:    [ 1.5 - 3.0 MVA ] -------> Requires On-Site Substation Feed
  Injection Molding / Stamping: [ 3.0 - 10.0+ MVA ] ------> Dedicated CFE High-Voltage Substation
```

### The "Spec Building" Power Trap

A frequent mistake made by corporate real estate teams is assuming that a newly constructed "Class A Shell" comes equipped with full manufacturing power. Standard speculative industrial buildings in Tijuana are delivered with **100 KVA to 250 KVA** of transformer capacity—sufficient for LED overhead lighting, executive offices, and loading dock operation, but inadequate for industrial machinery.

If your process requires 3 MVA of power and the developer has not pre-reserved grid capacity with CFE, securing that additional power requires:
- Formal CFE Feasibility Study (*Factibilidad de Servicio*).
- Network Impact Study (*Estudio de Impacto a la Red*).
- Acquisition of rights-of-way for primary feeder line extensions.
- Procurement, installation, and CFE commissioning of an on-site substation.

**Project Lead Time:** 12 to 18 months, with capital costs ranging from **$250,000 to $1.2M USD** depending on distance to the nearest CFE high-voltage transmission corridor.

## 4. Comparing Regional Power Capacity: Tijuana vs. Mexicali vs. Tecate

Power availability varies significantly across Baja California's major manufacturing hubs:

### Tijuana
- **Grid Infrastructure:** Connected to the Baja California Isolated Electrical System (SEBC). Rapid industrial expansion has put pressure on urban substations in Otay Mesa and Pacifico.
- **Power Strategy:** Top-tier parks (El Florido, FINSA, Pacifico) have constructed private step-down substations to offer pre-allocated medium-voltage power drops directly to tenants.
- **Tariff Benchmarks:** GDMTH industrial rates average **$0.118 to $0.145 USD per kWh**, with peak demand charges applied during summer months (June–September).
- **Submarket Insights:** Detailed submarket profiles and infrastructure maps are available in our comprehensive guide to [Tijuana Operations](/en/locations/tijuana).

### Mexicali
- **Grid Infrastructure:** Exceptional power grid stability anchored by the La Rosita Combined-Cycle Generation Plant (1,000+ MW) and Cerro Prieto Geothermal Field (570 MW), with direct WECC transmission interconnections into Southern California.
- **Power Advantage:** Mexicali industrial developments routinely accommodate 10 MVA to 50 MVA+ power drops with significantly shorter CFE approval timelines than Tijuana.
- **Best Suited For:** Heavy metal stamping, plastic injection molding, semiconductor assembly, and energy-intensive manufacturing.

### Tecate
- **Grid Infrastructure:** Moderate local power capacity; ideal for small-to-midsize plants requiring under 2 MVA. Competitive lease rates ($0.60–$0.75 NNN) help offset lower high-voltage availability.

## 5. Risk-Mitigation Checklist for Power & Real Estate Acquisition

To ensure your nearshore facility launches on schedule and within budget, follow this 5-step site selection framework:

```
  STEP 1: Calculate Total Electrical Connected Load & Peak Demand (KVA / MVA)
    |
  STEP 2: Request Written CFE Power Allocation Guarantee from Landlord / Developer
    |
  STEP 3: Perform Independent Engineering Audit of Substation & Transformer Specs
    |
  STEP 4: Negotiate "Power Energization Contingency Clause" in NNN Lease Agreement
    |
  STEP 5: Evaluate Redundant On-Site Backup (Generators / Battery Energy Storage)
```

1. **Calculate Peak Demand Early:** Determine both total connected load (HP/kW) and estimated operational peak demand (MVA) prior to initiating site searches.
2. **Verify "Delivered" Power vs. "Promised" Power:** Inspect official CFE *Oficio de Factibilidad* documentation verifying allocated MVA capacity for the specific parcel.
3. **Tie Lease Terms to Power Energization:** Negotiate lease clauses stipulating that rent commencement is contingent upon CFE's formal energization of the required power drop.
4. **Leverage Shelter Program Options:** Partnering with an established shelter provider allows US companies to utilize existing move-in-ready facilities with active CFE power drops, reducing operational startup timelines from 12 months to under 90 days.
5. **Install On-Site Energy Storage (BESS):** Integrate Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) or standby generators to protect sensitive automated assembly and cleanroom lines from grid voltage sags.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the average Class A industrial lease rate in Tijuana for 2026?
Class A industrial lease rates in Tijuana currently range from **$0.78 to $0.88 NNN per sq ft per month** ($9.36 to $10.56 per sq ft annually). Operating expenses (CAM) add an additional $0.08 to $0.14 per sq ft per month, covering park security, common area maintenance, insurance, and property taxes.

### How much electrical power is included in standard spec industrial buildings in Tijuana?
Standard speculative buildings in Tijuana are typically delivered with a base transformer capacity of **100 KVA to 250 KVA** per 50,000 sq ft shell. Operations requiring higher capacity (1.5 MVA to 10 MVA+) for injection molding, cleanrooms, or metal stamping must request dedicated CFE power drops or select parks with pre-commissioned substations.

### How long does it take to get a dedicated CFE power drop approved in Baja California?
If the industrial park has existing CFE substation infrastructure with pre-allocated capacity, energizing a medium-voltage connection takes **60 to 90 days**. However, if a new CFE substation or feeder line extension is required, permitting, construction, and grid commissioning can take **12 to 18 months**.

### Is electrical power availability better in Mexicali or Tijuana?
Mexicali generally offers greater raw electrical power grid capacity and faster high-voltage drop approvals due to the La Rosita Combined-Cycle plant (1,000+ MW), Cerro Prieto Geothermal complex (570 MW), and direct WECC grid interconnections. Tijuana offers unmatched proximity to San Diego and ports of entry, but high-power users in Tijuana must secure space in Class A parks with private substations.
