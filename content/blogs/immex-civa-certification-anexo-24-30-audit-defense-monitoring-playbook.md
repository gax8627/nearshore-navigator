# IMMEX & CIVA Compliance Playbook: Anexo 24 vs 30 Audit Defense, VAT Credit Protection & Monitoring (2026)

**A Forensic Guide for CFOs, General Counsel, and Trade Compliance Directors on Anexo 24/30 SCCC-VE Reconciliation, 16% VAT Cash Exposure Defense, Plan Maestro AI Triggers, and Shelter Pool Contagion**

*By [Denisse Martinez](/about/denisse-martinez), Founder & Principal Nearshore Advisor, Nearshore Navigator*

> [!NOTE]
> **Executive Enforcement Context & Operational Mandates:**
> - **The Fiscal Reality:** Under Mexico’s **SAT Plan Maestro 2026**, the Tax Administration Service (*Servicio de Administración Tributaria*) and the General Administration of Foreign Trade Audits (**AGACE**) have fully automated customs and tax enforcement. The era of retrospective paper audits has been replaced by continuous, real-time algorithmic targeting.
> - **The Core Vulnerability:** The **CIVA Certification (*Certificación en Materia de IVA e IEPS*)** grants IMMEX manufacturers a 100% fiscal credit on the 16% Value-Added Tax (VAT/IVA) due on temporary imports. However, this benefit hinges entirely on mathematical synchronization between internal ERP inventory engines (**Anexo 24**) and SAT's centralized tax credit ledger (**Anexo 30 / SCCC-VE**).
> - **The Liquidity Threat:** A single unresolved inventory variance, expired pedimento balance under **Ley Aduanera Article 108**, or un-transmitted discharge report triggers administrative suspension under **RGCE Rule 7.2.4**. Suspension eliminates the 16% VAT credit overnight, forcing foreign manufacturers to fund millions in upfront cash at ports of entry while fighting retroactive tax assessments.

## Executive Summary: The 16% VAT Liquidity Cliff Facing IMMEX Manufacturers

<div class="speakable-direct-answer direct-answer-capsule" id="direct-answer-summary" style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #0284c7; padding: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; border-radius: 0.375rem;">
  <p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #0f172a; margin: 0;">
    <strong>Direct Answer:</strong> The 16% VAT liquidity cliff occurs when Mexico's SAT suspends an IMMEX manufacturer's CIVA certification due to Anexo 24 and 30 discrepancies. This forces the company to immediately pay 16% VAT in cash on all temporary imports, freezing working capital and generating retroactive tax clawbacks with compound interest.
  </p>
</div>

Foreign manufacturing executives expanding to Mexico under the **IMMEX program (*Industria Manufacturera, Maquiladora y de Servicios de Exportación*)** frequently treat customs compliance as an administrative back-office function. In 2026, this assumption represents an existential financial risk.

When an enterprise operates under an IMMEX authorization without certification, Article 28-A of the Mexican VAT Law (*Ley del Impuesto al Valor Agregado - LIVA*) mandates the immediate cash payment of the **16% Value-Added Tax (IVA)** on every raw material, component, packaging item, and capital asset temporarily imported into Mexican territory. For a high-throughput advanced manufacturing facility, paying 16% cash at customs destroys operating margins and creates crippling working capital requirements.

To alleviate this burden, the Mexican Ministry of Finance (*Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público - SHCP*) established the **Certificación en Materia de IVA e IEPS (CIVA)**. Governed by Article 28-A of the LIVA, Article 15-A of the Excise Tax Law (*Ley del Impuesto Especial sobre Producción y Servicios - LIEPS*), and Chapter 7.1 of the General Foreign Trade Rules (*Reglas Generales de Comercio Exterior - RGCE*), CIVA grants certified companies an **instant 100% fiscal credit** applied directly to the customs declaration (*pedimento clave IN*). Under CIVA, no cash changes hands at the border; the 16% VAT liability is credited to an administrative account managed by SAT.

However, this credit is not an exemption—it is a conditional deferred liability. The legal validity of the credit relies entirely on proving to SAT that every temporarily imported item physically exited the country within statutory timeframes, either as part of a finished export product, through an authorized virtual transfer, or as certified scrap. 

```
                                    +-----------------------------------------+
                                    |       Temporary Import (Pedimento IN)   |
                                    |     Raw Materials Enter Mexico (Day 0)  |
                                    +--------------------+--------------------+
                                                         |
                                 16% VAT Deferred via CIVA Tax Credit
                                                         |
                                                         v
                                    +-----------------------------------------+
                                    |  SAT SCCC-VE / Anexo 30 Credit Ledger   |
                                    |      "Cargo" (Tax Liability Recorded)   |
                                    +--------------------+--------------------+
                                                         |
                              +--------------------------+--------------------------+
                              |                                                     |
        Transformation & Physical Export                      Failure to Discharge Within 18 Months
        Pedimento RT / H1 Cleared in Customs                  (Or Un-transmitted Anexo 30 Discharge)
                              |                                                     |
                              v                                                     v
    +------------------------------------+                +------------------------------------+
    | Anexo 30 Discharge (Abono Applied) |                |   CIVA Suspension & Tax Clawback   |
    |  VAT Liability Formally Dissolved  |                | 16% Cash Mandate + Fines + Penalties|
    +------------------------------------+                +------------------------------------+
```

### The Mathematics of CIVA Cancellation: Quantifying the Working Capital Freeze

When SAT’s audit division—the General Administration of Foreign Trade Audits (**AGACE**)—identifies material variances between an operator's internal inventory ledgers and SAT's centralized credit records, it initiates certification suspension proceedings under RGCE Rule 7.2.4. 

The financial consequence of CIVA cancellation is immediate, severe, and mathematically predictable. We express the total initial monthly cash exposure as:

$$E_{\text{monthly}} = V_{\text{import}} \times \tau_{\text{IVA}}$$

Where:
- $E_{\text{monthly}}$ is the immediate monthly cash outlay required for import VAT.
- $V_{\text{import}}$ is the monthly CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) customs commercial value of temporarily imported raw materials.
- $\tau_{\text{IVA}}$ is the statutory Mexican Value-Added Tax rate ($16\%$, or $0.16$).

Because Mexican tax refund petitions under Article 22 of the Federal Fiscal Code (*Código Fiscal de la Federación - CFF*) for non-certified entities routinely encounter administrative audit cycles lasting between **90 and 180 days**, the cumulative cash trapped at customs accumulates rapidly. The total rolling working capital disruption ($W_{\text{freeze}}$) across an average refund turnaround cycle of $N_{\text{months}}$ is formulated as:

$$W_{\text{freeze}} = \sum_{m=1}^{N_{\text{months}}} (V_{\text{import}, m} \times \tau_{\text{IVA}}) + C_{\text{carry}}$$

Where $C_{\text{carry}}$ represents the enterprise cost of capital required to finance the cash deficit:

$$C_{\text{carry}} = W_{\text{principal}} \times \left( \frac{r_{\text{annual}}}{12} \right) \times N_{\text{months}}$$

### Working Scenario: \$10,000,000 Monthly Temporary Import Run Rate

Consider a mid-sized automotive tier-1 or electronics contract manufacturing facility importing **\$10,000,000 USD** of electronic components, machined metals, and sub-assemblies per month under an IMMEX program in Tijuana, Ciudad Juárez, or Monterrey.

```
Monthly Import Run Rate (V_import):             $10,000,000 USD
Statutory Value-Added Tax Rate (tau_IVA):                16%
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Monthly Upfront VAT Cash Outlay (E_monthly):      $1,600,000 USD
Annual Cumulative VAT Cash Outlay (12 Months):  $19,200,000 USD
Rolling Cash Freeze (90-Day SAT Refund Lag):      $4,800,000 USD
```

## What Is CIVA Certification? Legal Framework Under Ley del IVA Art. 28-A & Ley del IEPS Art. 15-A

<div class="speakable-direct-answer direct-answer-capsule" id="direct-answer-civa" style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #0284c7; padding: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; border-radius: 0.375rem;">
  <p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #0f172a; margin: 0;">
    <strong>Direct Answer:</strong> CIVA is a specialized tax certification issued by SAT under Ley del IVA Article 28-A and Ley del IEPS Article 15-A. It grants eligible IMMEX companies an immediate 100% VAT credit on temporary imports across three modalities (A, AA, AAA) based on investment, headcount, and compliance history.
  </p>
</div>

The **Certificación en Materia de IVA e IEPS (CIVA)** was instituted to balance two conflicting governmental objectives: protecting Mexico's national tax base against the illegal domestic diversion of duty-free raw materials, while preserving the cash-flow competitiveness of the country's export manufacturing sector.

Prior to the 2014 Mexican Fiscal Reform, temporary imports entered Mexico completely exempt from VAT. When bad actors exploited this loophole by importing goods under fraudulent maquiladora programs and dumping them into the domestic retail market without paying taxes, the Mexican Congress amended **Article 28-A of the Ley del IVA** and **Article 15-A of the Ley del IEPS**. The revised statutes made all temporary imports legally subject to VAT and excise taxes, but created an administrative credit mechanism available exclusively to highly compliant companies certified by SAT.

### General Requirements for CIVA Accreditation (RGCE Rule 7.1.1)

To obtain and retain CIVA certification under the General Foreign Trade Rules, an IMMEX operating entity must prove continuous, unblemished fiscal and customs compliance. The core baseline criteria include:

1. **Active IMMEX Program:** A valid, active IMMEX authorization issued by the Ministry of Economy (*Secretaría de Economía - SE*), fully complying with annual minimum export thresholds (\$500,000 USD or 10% of total sales).
2. **Positive Tax Compliance Opinion (Opinión de Cumplimiento 32-D):** A pristine "Positiva" rating under Article 32-D of the CFF, verifying that federal taxes, social security dues (**IMSS**), housing fund contributions (**INFONAVIT**), and electronic tax filings are 100% current.
3. **No Blacklisted Taxpayers (EFOS / CFF Art. 69-B):** The enterprise, its shareholders, directors, and legal representatives must not appear on SAT's public lists of non-existent or invoice-simulating entities (*Empresas que Facturan Operaciones Simuladas*).
4. **Legitimate Physical Infrastructure:** Ownership or long-term operational lease of certified industrial facilities with real-time video surveillance, physical perimeter security, and inventory storage capacity commensurate with declared production volumes.
5. **Operational Anexo 24 System:** An operational, automated inventory control software capable of transmitting structured customs and production data to SAT according to official technical specifications.

### The Three CIVA Modalities: A, AA, and AAA

Under RGCE Rules 7.1.2 and 7.1.3, SAT stratifies CIVA certification into three distinct tiers based on the manufacturer’s corporate stability, fixed asset base, direct employment levels, and historical compliance tenure.

## The Anatomy of Anexo 24 (Internal ERP) vs Anexo 30 (SAT CCCyG) Reconciliation

<div class="speakable-direct-answer direct-answer-capsule" id="direct-answer-reconciliation" style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #0284c7; padding: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; border-radius: 0.375rem;">
  <p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #0f172a; margin: 0;">
    <strong>Direct Answer:</strong> Anexo 24 is an enterprise-maintained inventory control software tracking physical customs entries, transformations, and scrap. Anexo 30 is SAT’s external fiscal credit database (SCCC-VE) offsetting VAT. Discrepancies between them occur when physical factory discharges fail to match SAT’s digital tax accounts, triggering automated audit enforcement.
  </p>
</div>

The single most common point of operational failure in Mexican manufacturing compliance lies in the operational disconnect between **Anexo 24** and **Anexo 30**. Corporate controllers frequently assume that because their enterprise ERP (SAP, Oracle, NetSuite) tracks production orders, customs compliance is addressed. Under Mexican customs law, standard corporate ERPs are legally insufficient.

### Anexo 24: The Enterprise Customs Inventory Engine

Mandated by **Article 59, Section I of the Ley Aduanera** and structured under **Annex 24 of the RGCE**, this system is an automated, specialized customs inventory database. It does not replace corporate ERP; rather, it ingests ERP production data and couples it with official customs clearance declarations (*pedimentos*).

Anexo 24 must maintain three synchronized structural modules:
1. **Customs Inbound Receipts (*Entradas*):** Records every temporary import pedimento (claves IN, AF, V1), capturing entry date, Mexican Harmonized System classification (**Fracción Arancelaria**), commercial invoice value, physical unit of measure, tariff unit of measure (**UMT**), country of origin, and the statutory 18-month expiration date.
2. **Manufacturing Bills of Materials (BOM / *Descargos Estructurales*):** The engineering explosion matrices that define precisely how many grams, liters, centimeters, or units of raw material are consumed in the production of one unit of finished export goods, including legally recognized scrap (*merma*) and non-reusable waste (*desperdicio*).
3. **Customs Outbound Discharges (*Salidas*):** Records all export pedimentos (claves RT, H1, V1), destructive scrap declarations, or regularizations, linking outbound finished goods back to specific inbound pedimentos via a strict FIFO (*First-In, First-Out*) or specific batch-identification algorithm.

### Anexo 30: SAT's Centralized Credit and Guarantee Control System (CCCyG / SCCC-VE)

Governed by **Annex 30 of the RGCE** and **Rule 7.2.1**, Anexo 30 is not a corporate software application. It is the **Tax Credit and Guarantee Account Control System (*Sistema de Control de Cuentas de Créditos y Garantías - SCCC-VE*)**, an enterprise-level relational database hosted on SAT’s secure government servers.

Whenever a customs broker validates a temporary import pedimento (IN) under a CIVA-certified RFC, SAT’s customs clearance system automatically transmits a digital debt record—a **Cargo**—to the company's SCCC-VE account. This Cargo represents the exact 16% VAT value deferred at the port of entry.

To eliminate this fiscal liability, the IMMEX manufacturer must submit bi-weekly or monthly electronic discharge files—**Informes de Descargo**—directly to the SCCC-VE portal. When SAT’s system ingests and validates the Informe de Descargo, it applies a digital credit—an **Abono**—against the corresponding inbound pedimento batch, dissolving the deferred VAT liability.

### The "Submitting ≠ Validating" Pitfall: SCCC-VE Status Codes & Rejection Mechanics

A recurring, catastrophic misconception among manufacturing executives is assuming that transmitting an *Informe de Descargo* to SAT completes the compliance requirement. **Submitting a report is not equal to validating a control.**

Under RGCE Chapter 7.2, SAT's SCCC-VE architecture operates in distinct electronic stages:

1. **Acuse de Recepción (Transmission Acknowledgment):** Generated instantaneously upon uploading the discharge dataset. This receipt merely verifies that SAT's servers received the electronic file and verified the company's digital signature (e.firma). **It confers zero legal protection against tax omissions.**
2. **Acuse de Validación (Formal Validation Receipt):** Generated after SAT’s backend engines execute batch algorithmic cross-matching against historical inbound pedimentos (claves IN/AF). Only when the report status displays **"VÁLIDO" (Accepted)** is the 16% VAT tax credit formally applied and discharged.
3. **Claves de Rechazo (Automated Rejection Codes):** If SAT's algorithms detect structural discrepancies, the discharge is rejected in whole or in part. Common error codes include:
   - **Error 01 / Fracción No Correlativa:** The tariff fraction on the export pedimento does not correspond to an authorized BOM input fraction.
   - **Error 04 / Falta de Saldo Disponible:** Inbound pedimento batches have already been depleted by prior discharges, or the FIFO allocation algorithm desynchronized.
   - **Error 08 / Sobredescargo (Negative Balance):** The reported consumption exceeds the registered temporary import volume. SCCC-VE rejects negative balances and freezes the credit line.
   - **Error 12 / Pedimento Vencido:** The inbound pedimento batch exceeded the 18-month statutory holding limit prior to export date.
4. **The 30-Day Statutory Correction Window (RGCE Rule 7.2.1):** When a discharge report is rejected, companies are granted strictly **30 calendar days** to submit an extemporaneous corrected discharge (*informe rectificado*). If this window lapses without resolution, SAT automatically issues a digital pre-liquidation notice via Buzón Tributario, classifying the un-discharged balance as an unauthorized domestic diversion subject to immediate 16% VAT clawbacks.

## The 18-Month Statutory Clock: Temporary Import Expiration & Retroactive Tax Penalties

<div class="speakable-direct-answer direct-answer-capsule" id="direct-answer-clock" style="background-color: #f8fafc; border-left: 4px solid #0284c7; padding: 1.25rem; margin-bottom: 1.5rem; border-radius: 0.375rem;">
  <p style="font-size: 1.05rem; line-height: 1.6; color: #0f172a; margin: 0;">
    <strong>Direct Answer:</strong> Under Ley Aduanera Article 108, temporarily imported raw materials have a strict 18-month stay limit in Mexico. If goods exceed 18 months without formal export or change of regime, they become illegally present, triggering automatic 16% VAT clawbacks, severe customs fines, and asset seizure.
  </p>
</div>

Under Mexican customs law, temporary importation is a legal exception to national territorial sovereignty. The state permits foreign goods to enter without definitive duty and VAT payment exclusively under the condition that they are processed and returned abroad.

### Statutory Stay Limits Under Article 108 of the Ley Aduanera

Article 108 of the Mexican Customs Law (*Ley Aduanera*) strictly classifies the maximum time limits that temporarily imported goods may remain within Mexican territory:

1. **Paragraph I: 18-Month Stay Limit:**
   - Raw materials, parts, components, auxiliary supplies, labels, and packaging materials used directly in manufacturing export goods.
   - Fuels, lubricants, and cutting fluids consumed entirely during the manufacturing transformation process.
2. **Paragraph II: 2-Year Stay Limit:**
   - Shipping containers, trailer chassis, and intermodal logistics equipment.
3. **Paragraph III: Duration of the IMMEX Program:**
   - Production machinery, equipment, inspection instruments, tooling, molds, dies, replacement parts, and pollution-control equipment, provided the company maintains an active IMMEX program and CIVA certification.

### The Mechanics of the "Ticking Clock"

The 18-month clock commences on the exact calendar day that customs clearance is granted at the Mexican port of entry (the *Fecha de Pago / Despacho* stamped on the Pedimento IN). It does not pause when raw materials are delayed in local warehouses, held in production quarantine, or transferred between domestic facilities.

```
DAY 0: Customs Clearance (Pedimento IN) ---> SCCC-VE Cargo Applied
│
├── MONTHS 1–12: Ideal Operational Window
│   └── Material consumed in production; finished goods exported under Pedimento RT.
│   └── Anexo 30 Informe de Descargo submitted; Abono dissolves VAT credit.
│
├── MONTHS 13–15: Advanced Aging Zone (Yellow Alert)
│   └── Raw materials remain in warehouse due to demand shifts or engineering redesigns.
│   └── Compliance team must actively flag aging lots in Anexo 24.
│
├── MONTHS 16–17: Critical Intervention Window (Red Alert)
│   └── Immediate physical trace required. Options: expedited manufacturing,
│       virtual transfer (V1) to sister plant, or return abroad in same condition (H1).
│
└── DAY 541 (Month 18 + 1 Day): STATUTORY DEFAULT (LEGAL CLIFF)
    ├── Merchandise legally classified as "Ilegal Estancia en el País".
    ├── SCCC-VE automated engine classifies balance as "Saldo Vencido".
    ├── SAT Buzón Tributario notification generated under CFF Article 53-B.
    └── Precautionary seizure (PAMA) authorized under Ley Aduanera Article 151.
```

### Legal Consequences of Exceeding the 18-Month Window

On Day 541, un-discharged merchandise ceases to be covered by the IMMEX customs regime. The legal ramifications cascade across multiple statutes:

1. **Illegal Stay in the Country (*Ilegal Estancia*):** Under **Article 101 and Article 144 of the Ley Aduanera**, merchandise exceeding the authorized stay limit is legally presumed to have been diverted into the domestic economy without authorization.
2. **Revocation of the CIVA Tax Credit:** SAT's SCCC-VE system automatically cancels the 16% deferred VAT credit associated with the expired pedimento batch.
3. **Precautionary Administrative Seizure (PAMA):** Under **Article 151, Section III of the Ley Aduanera**, customs officials are legally mandated to execute an administrative customs seizure (*Procedimiento Administrativo en Materia Aduanera - PAMA*), taking physical custody of the expired raw materials or sealing warehouse storage bays.
4. **Retroactive Financial Penalties:** The enterprise is assessed the full underlying tax liability along with heavy punitive adjustments:
   - **Retroactive 16% VAT:** Calculated on the original customs value updated for inflation via the National Consumer Price Index (**INPC**).
   - **General Import Duty (IGI):** Applicable tariffs based on country of origin, losing USMCA preferential treatment due to procedural default.
   - **Late Payment Surcharges (*Recargos*):** Compounded monthly under CFF Article 21 (historically 1.47% per month, compounding to ~19.2% annually).
   - **Customs Penalties:** Fines ranging from **70% to 100% of unpaid taxes** under Article 178, Section I of the Ley Aduanera, or **130% to 150% of the commercial value of the merchandise** if import licenses were omitted.
5. **Joint Executive Liability (*Responsabilidad Solidaria*):** Under **Article 26, Section III of the CFF**, corporate directors, managing officers, and legal representatives of the Mexican operating entity bear personal financial liability for unpaid customs debts and penalties.

When inventory approaches the 15-month threshold, compliance officers must intervene proactively. Rectifying or regularizing inventory prior to formal SAT notification preserves corporate standing; waiting for a SAT audit notice eliminates the right to voluntary regularization.

### The 12 Operational Checkpoints

#### 1. VUCEM DataStage / Glosa Direct Extraction
Never rely solely on customs broker billing summaries. Export the raw **Glosa DataStage** electronic customs clearance files directly from the **VUCEM (*Ventanilla Única de Comercio Exterior Mexicana*)** portal. Compare VUCEM records line-by-line against internal inbound warehouse receiving sheets to ensure no pedimento was omitted or misattributed.

#### 2. Engineering BOM & Production Scrap Synchronization
Audit every Engineering Change Order (ECO) implemented on the manufacturing floor during the preceding month. Confirm that revised component explosion ratios, net usage quantities, and authorized scrap (*merma*) percentages have been updated in the Anexo 24 engine prior to generating export discharges.

#### 3. Certified Mass Balance & Scrap Destruction Audits
Reconcile physical scrap container weights against industrial recycler collection slips, certified destruction manifests (*Actas de Destrucción*), and virtual scrap pedimentos. Unregistered scrap is classified by SAT as illegal domestic diversion.

#### 4. The 15-Month Stay Aging Matrix Stratification
Filter active Anexo 24 open balances into three operational aging categories:
- **Green Zone (<12 Months):** Healthy operating inventory.
- **Yellow Zone (12 to 15 Months):** High-priority inventory requiring production scheduling or planned virtual transfer.
- **Critical Red Zone (>15 Months):** Emergency intervention inventory. Execute immediate expedited production, return abroad (Pedimento H1), or voluntary change of regime (Pedimento F4) before Day 540.

#### 5. Fixed Asset Physical Verification (Pedimentos Clave AF)
Conduct randomized physical audits of plant machinery, tooling, molds, and testing equipment. Verify that every physical asset on the production floor displays an indelible asset tag matching the commercial invoice, serial number, and **Pedimento de Importación Temporal de Activo Fijo (AF)** recorded in the permanent Anexo 24 registry.

#### 6. Tariff Classification (TIGIE) & UMT Conversion Audit
Verify that the Tariff Unit of Measure (**UMT**) declared on outbound export declarations matches the inbound declaration. A unit mismatch between kilograms, liters, and pieces will block SAT’s automated discharge engine.

#### 7. SCCC-VE Informe de Descargo Transmission
Compile and transmit the standardized electronic discharge datasets to SAT's CCCyG portal within the statutory monthly window. Ensure the transmission covers all completed export pedimentos (RT), foreign returns (H1), virtual transfers (V1), and regularizations.

#### 8. SCCC-VE Formal ACUSE and Exception Analysis
Do not assume transmission equals clearance. Download the official **Acuse de Aceptación** from the SCCC-VE portal. Cross-reference rejected records (*descargos no procesados*), identify root causes (syntax errors, expired balances, missing BOM linkages), and rectify discrepancies immediately.

#### 9. Bilateral Virtual Pedimento (V1) Confirmation
Audit all inter-company transfers, shelter transfers, and domestic tier-1 supplier deliveries conducted via virtual pedimentos (claves V1 and V5). Obtain the counterparty's validated pedimento copy and confirm that both import and export declarations were closed within the legally permitted timeframe under RGCE Rule 4.3.21.

#### 10. Complemento Carta Porte 3.1 Geolocation Audit
Cross-check carrier transportation invoices against inbound receiving logs. Confirm that digital transportation bills of lading contain valid **Complemento Carta Porte 3.1 UUIDs** and that destination GPS coordinates strictly match registered IMMEX facility authorizations.

#### 11. Daily Automated SAT Article 69-B (EFOS) Vendor Screening
Execute automated script queries matching the enterprise master vendor database against the official **Diario Oficial de la Federación (DOF)** blacklists of non-existent taxpayers. Block purchasing orders and accounts payable disbursements immediately upon an active EFOS alert.

#### 12. Real-Time IMSS Headcount and Opinión 32-D Health Checks
Download the official **Opinión de Cumplimiento de Obligaciones Fiscales (Article 32-D)** from the SAT portal to guarantee an unblemished "Positiva" rating. Simultaneously, verify that registered direct employee payroll records submitted via the **Sistema Único de Autodeterminación (SUA)** meet the minimum headcount threshold mandated for your specific CIVA modality (10 for A, 50 for AA, 250 for AAA).

### What happens if our CIVA certification is cancelled or suspended by SAT?
Upon CIVA cancellation or suspension under RGCE Rule 7.2.4, an IMMEX manufacturer immediately loses its 100% VAT/IEPS fiscal credit at Mexican customs. All subsequent temporary raw material imports require an upfront cash payment of 16% VAT at clearance. Additionally, SAT initiates an immediate audit of historical un-discharged SCCC-VE balances, assessing retroactive 16% VAT, inflationary updates (*actualización*), late-payment interest (*recargos*), and customs fines up to 100% of unpaid duties.

### Can we use temporary import VAT credits to offset domestic Mexican commercial sales?
No. CIVA tax credits managed under Anexo 30 apply exclusively to temporary imports (pedimento claves IN, AF, RT) that are transformed and subsequently exported from Mexico or transferred via authorized virtual pedimentos (V1). If temporarily imported goods are diverted into the Mexican domestic market, the company must execute a change of customs regime pedimento (clave F4), paying the 16% VAT in cash alongside applicable IGI customs duties and inflation surcharges.

### How long do we have to respond to an electronic audit notice through the Buzón Tributario?
Under Article 53-B of the Federal Fiscal Code (CFF), a taxpayer has strictly **10 business days** following digital notification in the Buzón Tributario to respond to an electronic audit provisional resolution (*resolución provisional*). Failure to submit complete documentary proof, corrected pedimentos (R1), and Anexo 24/30 reconciliation reports within this statutory window results in the pre-liquidated tax debt becoming definitive and legally enforceable.

### What is the difference between an R1 pedimento rectification and an A1 regularization?
An **R1 pedimento** is an administrative amendment used to correct data errors (such as tariff classification, commercial value, or unit of measure) on an existing customs declaration before or after clearance, provided customs faculties of inspection have not formally commenced. An **A1 regularization pedimento** under Ley Aduanera Article 101 is used when merchandise has exceeded its statutory 18-month stay limit or entered without legal clearance, converting illegal stay into definitive importation with full tax and fine payment.

### Why does SAT reject Anexo 30 discharge reports even when physical products were exported?
SAT's SCCC-VE automated validation engine automatically rejects discharge reports (*informes de descargo*) due to syntactic and structural discrepancies. Common failure causes include: mismatched tariff classification fractions between inbound and outbound declarations, tariff unit of measure (UMT) conversion errors, unregistered Bill of Materials (BOM) revisions, missing customs broker electronic signatures, and discharge transmissions submitted after the statutory monthly deadline.

### Can corporate directors be held personally liable for Anexo 24 inventory discrepancies in Mexico?
Yes. Under Article 26, Sections III and X of the Federal Fiscal Code (CFF - *Responsabilidad Solidaria*), legal representatives, general managers, and board members of Mexican operating entities bear joint and personal financial liability for unpaid customs duties, un-discharged 16% VAT, and associated penalties if the company fails to maintain required inventory control systems or defaults during an AGACE audit.
