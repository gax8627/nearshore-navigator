## 1. What is a Shelter Company? Definition & Legal Framework

A **shelter company** in Mexico is a specialized service provider that offers foreign manufacturers a legal, administrative, and regulatory umbrella. Under Mexican law and the federal **IMMEX program** (Fomento del Industria Manufacturera, Maquiladora y de Servicios de Exportación), the shelter firm acts as the local entity of record.

### Division of Operational Responsibilities

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                     YOUR MANUFACTURING COMPANY                    |
|  - Production Processes        - Equipment & Tooling Ownership     |
|  - Quality Control Standards   - Engineering & Design              |
|  - Intellectual Property       - Raw Material Specifications       |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
                                   |
                         OPERATIONAL SYNERGY
                                   v
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               BAJA CALIFORNIA SHELTER PROVIDER                    |
|  - HR, Recruiting & Payroll    - Mexican Tax & Legal Compliance    |
|  - IMMEX Customs Import/Export - Facility Permitting & EHS         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Under a standard **shelter services Baja California** contract:
- **The Shelter Provider Handles:** Human resources, talent acquisition, labor contract compliance, local payroll, environmental health & safety (EHS) permits, Mexican tax accounting, and import/export customs clearance.
- **You Control:** Manufacturing methodology, shop-floor management, product design, production volume, quality control, equipment maintenance, and plant culture.

## 2. 5 Strategic Benefits of Using Shelter Services in Baja California

### 1. Accelerated Setup Time (90-Day Ramp-Up)
Setting up a standalone Mexican legal entity (*Sociedad Anónima de Capital Variable* / S.A. de C.V.) requires tax registration, corporate bank accounts, environmental permits, and independent IMMEX approval—a process taking 6 to 12 months. A shelter partner grants immediate access to pre-approved IMMEX licenses, local tax IDs, and existing permits, slashing rollout time to 90 days.

### 2. Elimination of Mexican Legal Liability
Operating through a shelter provider shields your U.S. parent company from direct exposure to Mexican labor courts, tax audits by SAT (*Servicio de Administración Tributaria*), and local municipal litigation. The shelter serves as the primary legal entity of record.

### 3. Immediate 16% VAT Exemption via IMMEX
Without IMMEX and VAT certification, importing raw materials and manufacturing equipment into Mexico incurs a mandatory 16% Value-Added Tax (IVA). **Shelter services in Baja California** leverage the provider's certified IMMEX umbrella, enabling 100% tax-free temporary importation of equipment, machinery, and components.

### 4. Significant Cost Savings (40–60% Overhead Reduction)
Instead of hiring dedicated in-country accounting, legal, HR, and customs teams, manufacturers share administrative infrastructure across the shelter provider's network. Fully burdened direct labor in Baja California typically ranges from $4.50 to $6.50/hour (compared to $25.00+ in North America), unlocking substantial margin expansion.

### 5. Seamless Cross-Border Logistics
Baja California sits directly adjacent to Southern California, offering immediate access to the San Ysidro, Otay Mesa, and Calexico commercial ports of entry. Goods manufactured in Tijuana or Mexicali can be cross-docked into San Diego or Los Angeles distribution hubs within hours.

## 6. Financial Breakdown: Shelter Fee Structures Explained

Shelter providers in Baja California generally bill using one of two transparent cost models:

### Model A: Per-Headcount Hourly Markup (Most Common)
Under this model, the shelter provider charges a fixed fee per direct labor hour worked (e.g., **$1.25 to $2.25 per direct labor hour** on top of fully burdened payroll costs).
- **Best For:** Small to mid-sized plants (30 to 150 employees) looking for simple, scalable invoicing.

### Model B: Cost-Plus Administration Fee
Under a cost-plus model, the manufacturer pays actual pass-through costs (real estate lease, raw payroll, utilities) plus a fixed monthly management fee or percentage (typically **4% to 8% of total operating expenses**).
- **Best For:** Large-scale operations (150+ headcount) seeking maximum accounting transparency at volume.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What are shelter services in Baja California and how do they work?
Shelter services provide foreign companies with a complete legal, administrative, tax, and labor framework to operate in Mexico. The shelter firm acts as the official employer and importer of record, handling HR, payroll, IMMEX customs compliance, and municipal permits, while you maintain full control over product manufacturing, quality assurance, and equipment.

### How fast can a U.S. company start production under a shelter model?
Because shelter companies maintain active IMMEX permits, environmental certifications, and local tax IDs, foreign manufacturers can lease a facility and initiate production in **90 days**, compared to 6–12 months when establishing an independent entity.

### How does the IMMEX program save money on taxes?
Under the shelter's IMMEX certification, temporarily imported raw materials, machinery, tooling, and components are exempt from Mexico's **16% Value-Added Tax (IVA)** and standard import tariffs, provided the finished goods are exported within statutory timeframes.

### What is the difference between a shelter company and contract manufacturing?
Under a **shelter agreement**, you lease your own facility, bring in your own machinery, and manage your plant personnel directly, while the shelter handles admin/compliance. Under **contract manufacturing**, you outsource production to a third-party factory that produces goods to your specification with no facility investment on your end. Learn more about [contract manufacturing in Tijuana](/en/services/contract-manufacturing-tijuana).

### Who owns the machinery and intellectual property in a shelter arrangement?
You maintain **100% ownership** of all machinery, tooling, inventory, patents, and intellectual property. The shelter provider acts purely as an administrative partner and has no legal claim to your asset base.
