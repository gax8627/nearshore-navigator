# 2026 Industrial Park Vacancies in Tijuana & Monterrey: Substation Power Drops & Facility Selection

**A Strategic Site Selection Roadmap for C-Level Executives: Navigating Class A Lease Rates ($0.47–$0.85/SF NNN), CFE GDMTH Industrial Energy Tariffs, 10–50 MVA Power Drops, and Submarket Vacancies in Otay, El Florido, Hofusan, and Santa Catarina.**

*By Denisse Martinez, Industrial Real Estate & Supply Chain Lead, Nearshore Navigator*

## Executive Summary

The North American nearshoring boom has entered a mature, capital-intensive phase in 2026. While the initial wave of industrial relocation focused primarily on securing physical square footage, the defining constraint for C-level executives today is no longer building availability—it is **electrical power allocation and substation infrastructure**. 

Across Northern Mexico’s premier manufacturing epicenters, **Tijuana (Baja California)** and **Monterrey (Nuevo León)**, Class A industrial lease rates have stabilized within a broad benchmark band of **$0.47 to $0.85 per square foot per month NNN**. However, headline vacancy rates—holding at **1.2% to 1.8% in Tijuana** and **2.5% to 3.2% in Monterrey**—tell only half the story. 

A facility listed as "available" on commercial real estate databases may lack guaranteed megawatt (MW) capacity from Mexico's state utility, **Comisión Federal de Electricidad (CFE)**. For high-demand industrial processes such as automotive stamping, plastic injection molding, medical device molding, electronic surface-mount technology (SMT), and electric vehicle (EV) battery pack integration, procuring dedicated **10 MVA to 50 MVA electrical substation drops** has become the single longest lead-time item in plant deployment—frequently requiring **12 to 18 months** of engineering, CFE permitting, and transformer procurement.

This comprehensive guide provides Chief Operating Officers, VPs of Supply Chain, and Corporate Real Estate Directors with authoritative 2026 data on:
1. **Class A Lease Rate Benchmarks & Vacancy Realities** across key submarkets (Otay Mesa, El Florido, Hofusan, Santa Catarina, Apodaca, and Ciénega de Flores).
2. **CFE Energy Capacity & Industrial Tariff Structures** under High Voltage (DIST) and Medium Voltage (GDMTH) schedules, including peak capacity charges (~$2.12 MXN/kWh).
3. **Substation Power Drop Architecture (10–50 MVA)**, transformer lead times, and private CapEx allocation strategies.
4. **Real-Time Landed Cost Models**, anchored by a fully burdened assembly labor rate of **$7.84/hour**.
5. **The Executive 5-Phase Site Selection Checklist** for auditing utility capacity, legal compliance, and long-term operational scaling.

## 2. The Power Bottleneck: CFE GDMTH Tariffs & Electrical Substation Drops

For industrial site selectors, building footprint is secondary to power availability. A 150,000 SF Class A building is operationally useless if it carries only a baseline 500 kVA lighting connection when your process machinery requires **10 MW to 30 MW**.

### Deconstructing CFE Industrial Power Tariffs

Industrial electricity in Mexico is supplied by the federal entity **Comisión Federal de Electricidad (CFE)** under two primary industrial tariff brackets:

1. **GDMTH (Gran Demanda Media Tensión Horaria):** Medium Voltage tariff for industrial users connected at 13.8 kV or 34.5 kV with maximum demands exceeding 100 kW.
2. **DIST / DIT (Gran Demanda Alta Tensión - Subestación / Industria):** High Voltage tariffs for large-scale manufacturing facilities connected directly to CFE transmission networks at 115 kV or 230 kV.

```
                         CFE GDMTH TIME-OF-USE (TOU) BILLING ARCHITECTURE
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. BASE ENERGY (Off-Peak / Night): Lowest $/kWh rate; continuous baseline operation.             |
|  2. INTERMEDIATE ENERGY (Daytime): Standard $/kWh rate; standard shift operation.                |
|  3. PEAK ENERGY (Evening Hours): Premium $/kWh rate; critical load-management window.            |
|  4. PEAK CAPACITY DEMAND CHARGE: Monthly billing based on highest 15-min demand (~$2.12 MXN/kWh).|
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Blended Industrial Electricity Rates
Across Northern Mexico, blended GDMTH/DIST electricity costs range from **$0.105 to $0.145 per kWh USD**, depending on the peak/off-peak energy mix and local CFE transmission node charges. Live CFE capacity metrics benchmark peak capacity charges at **$2.12 MXN per kWh** (~$65.00 to $75.00 USD per kW-month of peak demand).

> [!TIP]
> **Operational Cost Saving:** Manufacturers operating multi-shift facilities can significantly lower blended energy costs by scheduling energy-intensive thermal processing, metal melting, or injection molding during **Base energy hours** (11:00 PM to 6:00 AM) to avoid peak capacity demand multipliers.

### The 10 MVA to 50 MVA Substation Drop Reality

Modern manufacturing plants require varying levels of electrical power density. A standard warehouse requires 50 to 100 VA per square foot, whereas an advanced manufacturing plant requires **150 to 350+ VA per square foot**.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  Facility Type               |  Power Density Benchmark    | Total Demand (100,000 SF Plant)      |
|  --------------------------- | --------------------------- | -----------------------------------  |
|  Dry Logistics / 3PL         |  30 - 60 VA / SF            | 0.5 MVA - 1.0 MVA                    |
|  Light Assembly / Kitting    |  80 - 120 VA / SF           | 1.2 MVA - 2.0 MVA                    |
|  Electronics / SMT           |  150 - 220 VA / SF          | 2.5 MVA - 3.5 MVA                    |
|  Plastic Injection / Stamping|  250 - 450+ VA / SF         | 4.0 MVA - 8.0+ MVA                   |
|  Heavy Industrial / Casting  |  500+ VA / SF               | 10.0 MVA - 50.0+ MVA                 |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### Procurement & Construction Lead Times for Substation Drops
When a site requires a dedicated high-voltage substation (e.g., 115 kV down to 13.8 kV/480V for 10–50 MVA capacity), manufacturers face a structured timeline:

```
Step 1: CFE Interconnection Feasibility Study (Factibilidad de Servicio) ---------> 3 - 5 Months
Step 2: Substation Design & CFE Engineering Approval (Proyecto Ejecutivo) --------> 2 - 4 Months
Step 3: Transformer & Switchgear Long-Lead Procurement ---------------------------> 8 - 12 Months
Step 4: Civil Works, Substation Construction & Line Interconnection -------------> 3 - 5 Months
Step 5: Testing, Commissioning & CFE Energization (Contrato de Suministro) ------> 1 - 2 Months
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TOTAL TIMELINE FOR 10-50 MVA DEDICATED SUBSTATION DROP:                          13 - 21 MONTHS
```

Because transformer manufacturing lead times globally remain stretched, securing a facility in an industrial park with **pre-allocated CFE power capacity or an existing substation** cuts 12 to 18 months off plant commissioning schedules.

## 5. The C-Suite Site Selection Checklist: Evaluating Facilities & Power Drops

To mitigate capital risk and avoid multi-month operational delays caused by utility bottlenecks, C-level executive site selection teams should execute this **5-Phase Facility Audit Protocol** before signing a binding Class A industrial lease.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                              5-PHASE EXECUTIVE SITE SELECTION PROTOCOL                            |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  [ Phase 1: Power & Grid Audit ] -------> CFE Factibilidad Letter / Available MVA / Transformer Lead|
|  [ Phase 2: Lease & Real Estate ] -------> NNN Base Rate / CAM Cap / TI Allowance / Expansion Rights |
|  [ Phase 3: Labor & HR Access ] ---------> $7.84/hr Baseline / Public Transit / Turnover Audits   |
|  [ Phase 4: Logistics & Customs ] -------> FAST Lanes / C-TPAT / IMMEX Dual-Entity Approval       |
|  [ Phase 5: Environmental Compliance ] -> MIA Permit / Uso de Suelo / CFE Substation Interconnect   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Phase 1: Electrical Power & Substation Audit
- [ ] **Verify CFE Factibilidad de Servicio Letter:** Request the official CFE document from the park developer confirming available capacity (in MVA/MW) at the specific building transformer tap.
- [ ] **Audit Substation Drop Infrastructure:** Confirm whether the building connects to a shared park substation or requires a dedicated high-voltage drop (115 kV).
- [ ] **Transformer Lead Time & CapEx Allocation:** Clarify if the landlord provides the step-down transformer (13.8 kV to 480V/220V) as part of Tenant Improvements (TI) or if it must be capital-funded by the tenant.
- [ ] **Emergency Backup & Solar Allowance:** Check park rules for installing natural gas generators or rooftop photovoltaic (PV) solar panels (up to the 500 kW net-metering limit without CRE generation permits).

### Q2: Why is electrical power capacity the primary bottleneck for industrial site selection in Mexico?
**Answer:** While shell space availability has slightly increased due to active development, securing high-voltage power allocation from **CFE (Comisión Federal de Electricidad)** remains the critical path item. Energy-intensive manufacturing operations (plastics injection molding, metal stamping, EV battery assembly, electronics) require **10 MVA to 50 MVA substation drops**. Lead times for CFE power drop approvals and transformer substation procurement currently range between **12 and 18 months**, making power-ready industrial parks the single highest priority for site selection.

### Q3: How do CFE industrial power tariffs (GDMTH vs. DIST) work for foreign manufacturers?
**Answer:** CFE supplies industrial power under Medium Voltage (**GDMTH - Gran Demanda Media Tensión Horaria**) and High Voltage (**DIST/DIT**) tariffs. Blended energy rates across Northern Mexico range between **$0.105 and $0.145 per kWh USD**. GDMTH tariffs utilize time-of-use (TOU) pricing structured around Base, Intermediate, and Peak hours, alongside monthly maximum demand capacity charges (~$2.12 MXN/kWh peak capacity). Strategic load-shifting and dedicated private substations significantly lower effective electricity costs.

### Q4: What is the fully burdened labor rate benchmark for industrial assembly in 2026?
**Answer:** As of 2026, the fully burdened labor rate for entry-to-mid level direct manufacturing operators in Tijuana and Monterrey is **$7.84 per hour**. This fully burdened cost includes the Northern Border Zone minimum wage baseline, mandatory IMSS social security contributions, INFONAVIT housing fund deposits, 13th-month aguinaldo bonus, vacation premiums, state payroll taxes, and employer fringe benefits such as subsidized cafeteria services and transportation.

### Q5: What are the primary differences between Hofusan Industrial Park in Monterrey and El Florido in Tijuana?
**Answer:** Hofusan Industrial Park in Monterrey (Pesquería corridor) is an 850-hectare master-planned hub specializing in Asian FDI (Chinese and Korean OEMs/Tier-1 suppliers) with expansive land plots, lower lease rates (**$0.47–$0.58/SF NNN**), and dedicated 50+ MVA electrical substation infrastructure. El Florido in Tijuana is an established urban industrial corridor dominated by medical devices, aerospace, and precision electronics assembly with premium lease rates (**$0.78–$0.85/SF NNN**), immediate proximity to the San Diego/Otay Mesa commercial port of entry, and tight vacancy (under 1.5%).
